# SkyPath — User Research Plan

## 1. Research Overview

SkyPath is a user experience concept for a modern airline booking and travel-management platform.

The competitive research established that flight-booking platforms already provide extensive search and comparison functionality. The next research stage therefore focuses on understanding the human side of the problem:

- How travellers search for flights
- How they compare alternatives
- What information influences their decisions
- Where uncertainty occurs
- What causes friction during booking
- What information users need before payment
- What users expect after completing a booking

The research is intended to inform the interaction model, information architecture, personas, user flows and subsequent interface design.

---

## 2. Research Problem

Flight booking is not simply a search task.

Travellers must evaluate multiple variables simultaneously, including:

- Price
- Departure and arrival time
- Journey duration
- Number of stops
- Baggage allowance
- Fare flexibility
- Seat options
- Additional services
- Cancellation and change conditions

The research therefore investigates how these variables affect user decision-making and where a booking interface can reduce unnecessary cognitive effort.

---

## 3. Research Approach

The SkyPath research framework combines:

### Secondary Research

Review of existing airline and travel-booking experiences to identify established interaction patterns, strengths and gaps.

### Exploratory User Research

A planned qualitative research approach focused on traveller behaviours, expectations, frustrations and decision-making.

### Behavioural Analysis

Mapping the user's journey from initial travel intent through booking and post-booking management.

### Synthesis

Research observations are organised into:

- Themes
- Behaviour patterns
- Pain points
- Needs
- Opportunities
- Design implications

---

## 4. Research Questions

The research is structured around the following questions:

1. How do travellers currently search for flights?
2. Which factors influence flight selection?
3. How do users compare multiple flight options?
4. How important is the total cost compared with the advertised fare?
5. How do travellers understand different fare types?
6. Which information creates uncertainty during booking?
7. Which parts of passenger information entry create friction?
8. How do users approach baggage and seat-selection decisions?
9. What causes hesitation before payment?
10. What information do travellers expect immediately after booking?
11. What post-booking functions are most valuable?
12. How can a booking interface support confidence without overwhelming the user?

---

## 5. Target User Context

The research focuses primarily on travellers who use digital platforms to search for and book flights.

Relevant contexts include:

- Students travelling between cities or countries
- Young professionals travelling for work or personal reasons
- Frequent travellers
- Occasional travellers
- Budget-conscious travellers
- Travellers comparing multiple airlines

The project intentionally considers different levels of travel experience because booking behaviour can vary significantly between frequent and occasional travellers.

---

## 6. Key Research Areas

### Flight Discovery

Understanding how users begin a search and which constraints they establish first.

### Flight Comparison

Understanding how users compare price, time, stops and other attributes.

### Fare Selection

Understanding how users interpret fare differences and included services.

### Passenger Information

Understanding where data entry becomes repetitive or confusing.

### Optional Services

Understanding how users evaluate seats, baggage and additional services.

### Payment

Understanding the information required before users feel comfortable completing a transaction.

### Post-Booking

Understanding what users need after payment and confirmation.

## 8. Ethical and Data Considerations

No personally identifiable participant information should be stored in the public repository.

Where research is conducted, participant identities should be anonymised.

Research documentation should distinguish clearly between:

- Observed behaviour
- Participant statements
- Secondary research findings
- Design assumptions
- Product hypotheses

This prevents assumptions from being presented as empirical evidence.

---

## 9. Research-to-Design Relationship

The research is not treated as a standalone documentation exercise.

Findings are expected to influence:

**Research**

→

**Insights**

→

**Personas**

→

**Problem Definition**

→

**Information Architecture**

→

**User Flows**

→

**Wireframes**

→

**Interface Design**

→

**Validation**

Each major design decision should be traceable to either a research insight, usability principle, product requirement or clearly identified design hypothesis.

---

## 10. Expected Research Outcome

The primary objective is to determine how SkyPath can reduce uncertainty and cognitive effort during flight booking.

The research should help answer a central design question:

> How might a flight-booking experience help travellers move from multiple available options to a confident booking decision with minimal unnecessary effort?

---

## 11. Research Limitations

The research framework does not assume that all travellers behave identically.

Flight-booking behaviour can vary according to:

- Travel frequency
- Budget
- Trip purpose
- Destination
- Age
- Digital familiarity
- Airline preference
- Baggage requirements
- Flexibility

Therefore, findings should be interpreted within the context of the participants and scenarios actually studied.
