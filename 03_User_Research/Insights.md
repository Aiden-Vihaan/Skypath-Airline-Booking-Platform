# SkyPath — User Research Insights

## 1. Overview

The SkyPath user research explores how travellers search for flights, compare available options, evaluate fares, complete the booking process and manage their travel after booking.

The purpose of the synthesis is to identify the key behavioural patterns, user needs and experience gaps that can influence the design of the SkyPath flight-booking experience.

The insights below are used as a foundation for persona development, journey mapping, information architecture, user flows and interface design.

---

## 2. Insight: Flight Selection Is More Than Finding the Lowest Price

Travellers do not necessarily select the cheapest available flight.

The decision can depend on several factors, including:

- Total price
- Departure time
- Arrival time
- Journey duration
- Number of stops
- Airline
- Airport
- Baggage allowance
- Fare flexibility
- Seat availability

### Design implication

The interface should help users evaluate multiple important attributes together rather than making price the only prominent comparison factor.

---

## 3. Insight: Total Cost Matters More Than the Initial Fare

An attractive initial fare can become less appealing when additional costs are introduced during the booking process.

Potential additional costs include:

- Baggage
- Seat selection
- Fare upgrades
- Additional services
- Change or flexibility options

### Design implication

SkyPath should maintain clear visibility of the evolving booking total and communicate what is included within each selected fare.

---

## 4. Insight: Fare Differences Can Create Uncertainty

Different fare categories can be difficult to compare when users are shown only a fare name and price.

Travellers need to understand the practical difference between available options.

Important considerations include:

- Baggage allowance
- Cancellation conditions
- Change flexibility
- Seat benefits
- Included services
- Restrictions

### Design implication

Fare options should communicate differences using clear, comparable information rather than relying on unfamiliar fare terminology.

---

## 5. Insight: Flight Comparison Can Create Cognitive Load

A flight-search result can contain a large amount of information at once.

Users may need to compare:

- Multiple flights
- Departure times
- Arrival times
- Duration
- Stops
- Prices
- Airlines
- Baggage
- Fare conditions

When these details are presented without a clear hierarchy, comparison can become difficult.

### Design implication

SkyPath should use strong information hierarchy, grouping and progressive disclosure to make complex information easier to process.

---

## 6. Insight: Users Need Continuous Booking Context

The booking journey contains multiple connected decisions.

A user may select:

1. A flight
2. A fare
3. Passenger details
4. Baggage
5. Seat
6. Additional services
7. Payment

Losing context between these stages can increase uncertainty and force users to remember previous selections.

### Design implication

Important booking information should remain visible or easily accessible throughout the booking journey.

---

## 7. Insight: Optional Services Should Remain Clearly Optional

Seats, baggage and additional services can be useful, but they are not equally important to every traveller.

When optional services are presented as though they are mandatory, the booking experience can feel more complicated.

### Design implication

SkyPath should clearly separate:

**Required booking information**

from

**Optional services**

Users should be able to continue without purchasing unnecessary additions.

---

## 8. Insight: Repetitive Passenger Information Can Increase Friction

Passenger information is necessary for completing a booking, but repeated manual entry can increase effort and create opportunities for errors.

### Design implication

A future SkyPath experience could support reusable traveller information while maintaining user control over stored data.

The design should balance convenience with privacy and transparency.

---

## 9. Insight: Users Need Confidence Before Payment

The final payment stage represents a high-confidence decision point.

Before completing the transaction, users may want to verify:

- Flight
- Date
- Passenger information
- Fare
- Baggage
- Seat
- Additional services
- Final price
- Important restrictions

### Design implication

The booking review stage should provide a clear summary of all major decisions before payment.

---

## 10. Insight: Transparency Builds Trust

Trust in a booking platform is influenced by how clearly the system communicates important information.

Users need confidence that:

- The displayed price is understandable
- Additional costs are visible
- Fare conditions are accessible
- Booking information is correct
- The final amount is clear
- The next step is predictable

### Design implication

SkyPath should avoid hiding important decision-making information behind unnecessary interactions.

---

## 11. Insight: The Experience Continues After Booking

Completing payment does not represent the end of the traveller's journey.

After booking, users may need access to:

- Booking reference
- Itinerary
- Flight information
- Seat information
- Baggage details
- Check-in
- Flight status
- Changes or cancellation

### Design implication

The confirmation experience should transition naturally into post-booking access and management.

---

## 12. Insight: Traveller Priorities Are Not Universal

Different travellers can approach the same booking task differently.

For example:

- A budget-conscious traveller may prioritise price.
- A business traveller may prioritise time.
- A family traveller may prioritise baggage and seating.
- A frequent traveller may prioritise speed and convenience.
- A student traveller may prioritise overall affordability.

### Design implication

SkyPath should support different decision priorities rather than assuming that all users evaluate flights using the same criteria.

---

# 13. Major Research Themes

The insights can be grouped into six major themes.

## Theme 1 — Decision Confidence

Users need enough relevant information to make a confident booking decision.

## Theme 2 — Price Transparency

Users need to understand the relationship between the advertised fare and the final cost.

## Theme 3 — Effective Comparison

Users need to compare important flight attributes without excessive effort.

## Theme 4 — Cognitive Load

Complex information should be structured and prioritised rather than simply removed.

## Theme 5 — Booking Continuity

Important selections should remain connected throughout the booking journey.

## Theme 6 — Post-Booking Support

The product should continue to provide value after the transaction is completed.

---

# 14. Key User Needs

The research synthesis identifies the following high-level needs:

1. Understand flight options quickly.
2. Compare flights using meaningful criteria.
3. Understand the actual cost of a booking.
4. Understand what each fare includes.
5. Avoid unnecessary complexity.
6. Review important decisions before payment.
7. Complete passenger information efficiently.
8. Maintain control over optional services.
9. Access booking information after payment.
10. Feel confident throughout the booking process.

---

# 15. Opportunity Areas

The research insights indicate several potential opportunities for SkyPath.

### Opportunity 01 — Smarter Flight Comparison

Provide a structured comparison experience that prioritises the attributes most relevant to the traveller.

### Opportunity 02 — Transparent Pricing

Make the relationship between base fare, additional services and final cost easy to understand.

### Opportunity 03 — Clear Fare Comparison

Explain the practical differences between fare options.

### Opportunity 04 — Progressive Information

Reveal detailed information when it becomes relevant instead of presenting everything simultaneously.

### Opportunity 05 — Persistent Booking Summary

Maintain a clear representation of the user's current booking decisions.

### Opportunity 06 — Simplified Optional Services

Allow users to select baggage, seats and other services without making the core booking flow unnecessarily complex.

### Opportunity 07 — Confident Checkout

Provide a comprehensive but easy-to-scan review before payment.

### Opportunity 08 — Post-Booking Management

Extend the experience beyond confirmation through accessible booking and travel information.

---

# 16. Research Hypotheses

The following hypotheses can be validated through usability testing and further user research.

### H1

A structured flight comparison interface will help users evaluate alternatives more efficiently.

### H2

Clear fare comparisons will reduce uncertainty when users choose between different fare categories.

### H3

Early visibility of the evolving total cost will improve price confidence.

### H4

A persistent booking summary will reduce the cognitive effort required to remember previous selections.

### H5

Separating optional services from mandatory booking information will make the booking process easier to understand.

### H6

A structured review stage will increase confidence immediately before payment.

### H7

Providing accessible post-booking information will improve continuity after the transaction.

These insights represent the current research synthesis and should be interpreted according to the evidence available during the project.

Where an insight is based on secondary research, competitive observation or UX principles, it should not be represented as direct participant evidence.

Unvalidated assumptions should remain hypotheses until they are tested.

---

# 19. Core Research Insight

The strongest overarching insight is that flight booking is not simply an information-search task.

It is a **decision-support problem**.

Travellers are presented with many possible options, but the real challenge is understanding the trade-offs between those options and selecting the one that best fits their circumstances.

Therefore, the central design direction for SkyPath is:

> **Help travellers understand the important trade-offs between flight options so they can make a confident booking decision with less cognitive and interactional effort.**
