# SkyPath — Participants

## 1. Purpose

The participant framework defines the characteristics of people who are relevant to the SkyPath user research.

The objective is to capture a range of flight-booking experiences rather than focus on a single type of traveller.

Participant information should be anonymised in the project repository.

---

# 2. Target Participant Profile

The research focuses on adults who have experience searching for, comparing or booking flights through digital platforms.

Relevant characteristics include:

- Previous online flight-search experience
- Previous online booking experience
- Different travel frequencies
- Different travel purposes
- Different levels of digital familiarity
- Different priorities when selecting flights

---

# 3. Participant Segmentation

Participants can be broadly organised into three behavioural groups.

## Segment A — Occasional Travellers

### Typical Characteristics

- Travel by air infrequently
- May require more guidance during booking
- May be more sensitive to unfamiliar terminology
- May spend more time comparing options

### Research Relevance

Occasional travellers can reveal discoverability, comprehension and confidence issues.

---

## Segment B — Regular Travellers

### Typical Characteristics

- Travel several times a year
- Familiar with common airline terminology
- Comfortable comparing flights
- Often have established booking habits

### Research Relevance

Regular travellers can reveal efficiency issues and repetitive interactions.

---

## Segment C — Frequent Travellers

### Typical Characteristics

- Travel frequently
- Often prioritise time and convenience
- May have strong airline or loyalty preferences
- Usually understand common booking patterns

### Research Relevance

Frequent travellers can reveal opportunities for:

- Efficiency
- Personalisation
- Saved information
- Faster comparison
- Booking management

---

---

# 5. Recommended Participant Diversity

Where research recruitment permits, the sample should contain variation across:

| Dimension | Desired Variation |
|---|---|
| Travel frequency | Occasional → Frequent |
| Trip purpose | Education → Work → Leisure → Family |
| Booking experience | Beginner → Experienced |
| Price sensitivity | Lower → Higher |
| Flexibility | Fixed → Flexible |
| Booking style | Direct airline → Metasearch → Multiple platforms |

The purpose of this variation is to identify behavioural patterns that may not appear within a homogeneous sample.

---

# 6. Participant Anonymisation

Participant identities should not be stored publicly unless explicit permission has been obtained.

Use identifiers such as:

- P01
- P02
- P03
- P04
- P05

rather than:

- Full name
- Email address
- Phone number
- Home address
- Passport information
- Booking reference

---

---

# 8. Research Evidence Classification

Participant information should distinguish between different evidence levels.

### Direct Evidence

Information explicitly stated by the participant.

### Observed Behaviour

Actions directly observed during a research session.

### Researcher Interpretation

An interpretation derived from participant behaviour.

### Hypothesis

A proposed explanation that requires further validation.

This distinction is important when converting participant information into design decisions.

---

# 9. Recruitment Considerations

Recruitment should prioritise relevance to the research problem rather than simply maximising participant count.

Potential recruitment channels include:

- University communities
- Professional networks
- Travel communities
- Personal networks
- Online communities

Recruitment messages should clearly communicate:

- Research purpose
- Approximate duration
- Voluntary nature of participation
- Privacy expectations
- Whether the session will be recorded

---

# 10. Participant Consent

Before an interview or research session, participants should understand:

- Why the research is being conducted
- How their responses will be used
- Whether the session will be recorded
- How their identity will be protected
- That participation is voluntary
- That they may stop participating
