# SkyPath — User Interview Guide

## 1. Interview Purpose

The purpose of the interview is to understand how travellers approach flight search and booking in their own words.

The interview focuses on behaviour, decision-making, expectations, frustrations and confidence rather than evaluating a particular interface.

The discussion should encourage participants to describe actual experiences rather than hypothetical opinions wherever possible.

---

# 2. Research Objectives

The interview is designed to investigate:

- Flight-search behaviour
- Flight-comparison behaviour
- Decision-making factors
- Price perception
- Fare comprehension
- Baggage and seat-selection behaviour
- Passenger-information entry
- Booking friction
- Payment confidence
- Post-booking expectations

---

# 3. Interview Format

**Recommended duration:** 30–45 minutes

**Format:** Semi-structured interview

**Approach:** Open-ended questioning with follow-up prompts

The interviewer should avoid leading participants toward a particular answer.

Participants should be encouraged to explain:

- What they did
- Why they did it
- What they expected
- What confused them
- What they considered important
- What they would change

---

# 4. Opening Script

Thank you for taking part in this research.

We are exploring how people search for and book flights online. We are interested in your actual experiences, habits and expectations.

There are no right or wrong answers. We are interested in how you personally approach the process.

You do not need to evaluate the researcher or provide answers that you think we want to hear.

---

# 5. Warm-Up Questions

### Q1. Tell me about the last time you travelled by air.

**Follow-up prompts:**

- Where were you travelling?
- What was the purpose of the trip?
- How far in advance did you plan?
- Who were you travelling with?

---

### Q2. How did you search for your flight?

**Follow-up prompts:**

- Which website or application did you use?
- Did you use more than one platform?
- Why did you choose that platform?

---

### Q3. Walk me through what you remember doing from the beginning of your search until you selected a flight.

**Follow-up prompts:**

- What did you look at first?
- What did you compare?
- Did you change your search?
- Did you return to previous results?

# 17. Interviewer Notes

The interviewer should distinguish between:

### Behaviour

What the participant actually did.

### Motivation

Why the participant behaved that way.

### Pain Point

Something that caused difficulty, frustration or uncertainty.

### Need

An underlying requirement revealed by the behaviour.

### Expectation

What the participant assumed the system would provide.

### Opportunity

A potential area where the experience could be improved.

---

# 18. Avoiding Leading Questions

The interviewer should avoid questions such as:

- "Don't you think this process is confusing?"
- "Wouldn't you prefer a simpler comparison?"
- "Would this feature make booking easier?"

Instead, use neutral questions such as:

- "How did you find that process?"
- "What did you expect to happen?"
- "What did you do next?"
- "What made you choose that option?"
- "Was there anything that made you hesitate?"

---

# 19. Evidence Classification

Research notes should classify observations according to their evidence level.

| Evidence Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observed | Directly observed behaviour |
| Reported | Participant explicitly described the behaviour |
| Inferred | Interpretation based on multiple observations |
| Hypothesis | Assumption requiring validation |
| Secondary | Evidence obtained from existing research or competitive analysis |

This classification prevents assumptions from being presented as direct user evidence.

---

# 20. Synthesis Preparation

Following the interviews, observations should be organised into recurring themes.

Potential themes include:

- Price sensitivity
- Time sensitivity
- Fare confusion
- Baggage concerns
- Comparison difficulty
- Information overload
- Repetitive data entry
- Trust
- Decision confidence
- Post-booking needs

These themes will feed into the subsequent affinity mapping and research synthesis process.

---

# 21. Research-to-Design Traceability

Interview findings should eventually connect to specific design decisions.

The intended chain is:

**Participant evidence**

→

**Behavioural pattern**

→

**Research insight**

→

**User need**

→

**Design opportunity**

→

**Interface decision**

→

**Validation**

This traceability is important because it demonstrates that the SkyPath interface is derived from a structured UX process rather than visual preference alone.
