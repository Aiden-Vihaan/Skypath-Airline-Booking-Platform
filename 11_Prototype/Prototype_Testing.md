# SkyPath — Prototype Testing

## 1. Overview

Prototype testing is used to evaluate whether the SkyPath airline booking experience is understandable, efficient, accessible, and aligned with the needs identified during the user research phase.

The testing focuses on the high-fidelity prototype and the most important traveller tasks.

The purpose is not to validate visual preference alone, but to identify usability problems, interaction issues, information gaps, and points of uncertainty before implementation.

---

## 2. Testing Objectives

The prototype testing process aims to evaluate:

- Ease of flight search.
- Flight comparison behaviour.
- Understanding of fare differences.
- Clarity of baggage information.
- Ease of seat selection.
- Understanding of additional services.
- Transparency of pricing.
- Passenger information entry.
- Booking review behaviour.
- Payment confidence.
- Booking confirmation clarity.
- Post-booking navigation.
- Refund and cancellation understanding.
- Mobile usability.
- Accessibility of important interactions.

---

## 3. Key Research Questions

The following questions guide prototype evaluation:

1. Can users search for a flight without assistance?
2. Can users understand and compare available flight options?
3. Can users distinguish between different fare types?
4. Do users understand what baggage is included?
5. Can users add additional baggage confidently?
6. Can users select an appropriate seat?
7. Do users understand when an additional service costs money?
8. Can users identify the final booking price before payment?
9. Can users review and edit their booking before confirmation?
10. Can users recover from common errors?
11. Do users understand the payment state?
12. Is the booking confirmation sufficiently clear?
13. Can users find post-booking actions?
14. Can users understand cancellation and refund information?
15. Can users complete the main journey comfortably on mobile?

---

## 4. Testing Method

### Method

Moderated usability testing using the high-fidelity interactive prototype.

### Prototype

SkyPath high-fidelity Figma prototype.

### Testing Format

Participants are asked to complete realistic airline-booking scenarios while interacting with the prototype.

The facilitator observes:

- Task completion.
- Navigation behaviour.
- Hesitation.
- Misinterpretation.
- Errors.
- Questions.
- Backtracking.
- Unexpected interaction patterns.
- Confidence.

---

## 8. Task 01 — Search for a Flight

### Scenario

You are planning a trip and want to find a suitable flight for your journey.

### Task

Search for a flight between the required origin and destination for the desired travel dates.

### Observe

- Does the participant understand where to begin?
- Can they identify origin and destination fields?
- Can they select dates?
- Can they adjust passenger count?
- Do they understand the Search action?

### Success Criteria

The participant reaches the flight-results page without assistance.

---

## 9. Task 02 — Compare Flight Options

### Scenario

Several flights are available and you want to choose the option that best fits your needs.

### Task

Compare available flights and select the most suitable option.

### Observe

- What information does the participant look at first?
- Can they compare price and duration?
- Do they understand stops?
- Do they notice baggage information?
- Can they identify the recommended option?

### Success Criteria

The participant can explain why they selected the chosen flight.

---

## 10. Task 03 — Select a Fare

### Scenario

You have selected a flight but need to decide which fare is appropriate.

### Task

Compare the available fares and select one.

### Observe

- Can participants understand fare differences?
- Do they notice baggage allowance?
- Do they understand cancellation/change conditions?
- Are benefits easy to compare?

### Success Criteria

The participant selects a fare and can explain the major differences between the available options.

---

## 11. Task 04 — Enter Passenger Information

### Scenario

You are ready to continue with the booking and need to enter traveller details.

### Task

Complete the passenger information form.

### Observe

- Are field labels clear?
- Are required fields obvious?
- Do participants understand validation messages?
- Can they recover from an incorrect entry?

### Success Criteria

The participant completes the form without facilitator intervention.

---

## 12. Task 05 — Select a Seat

### Scenario

You would like to choose a preferred seat for the journey.

### Task

Open the seat map and select an available seat.

### Observe

- Can participants understand the seat map?
- Can they identify available and unavailable seats?
- Do they understand paid versus included seats?
- Can they identify their selected seat?

### Success Criteria

The participant successfully selects and confirms an appropriate seat.

---

## 13. Task 06 — Manage Baggage

### Scenario

You are travelling with additional luggage and need to check whether your fare includes enough baggage.

### Task

Review your baggage allowance and add additional baggage if necessary.

### Observe

- Can participants find baggage information?
- Do they understand included baggage?
- Do they understand weight limits?
- Do they understand additional costs?
- Is the updated price visible?

### Success Criteria

The participant successfully reviews and modifies baggage without confusion.

---

## 14. Task 07 — Review the Booking

### Scenario

You are about to pay and want to make sure everything is correct.

### Task

Review the complete booking before proceeding to payment.

### Observe

- Can participants locate all important information?
- Can they identify the total price?
- Can they find baggage and seat selections?
- Can they identify how to edit information?

### Success Criteria

The participant can verify the booking and identify any information they would change.

---

## 15. Task 08 — Complete Payment

### Scenario

You are satisfied with the booking and want to complete the purchase.

### Task

Proceed through the payment interaction.

### Observe

- Is the payment action clear?
- Does the processing state provide sufficient feedback?
- Does the participant understand whether payment succeeded?
- Is duplicate submission prevented?

### Success Criteria

The participant understands the payment state and reaches confirmation.

---

## 16. Task 09 — Find Booking Confirmation

### Scenario

Your booking has been completed and you want to verify the trip details.

### Task

Review the booking confirmation.

### Observe

- Can participants identify the booking reference?
- Can they find flight information?
- Can they locate baggage and seat information?
- Can they find the next relevant action?

### Success Criteria

The participant can confirm that the booking was successfully completed.

---

## 17. Task 10 — Manage an Existing Booking

### Scenario

You have already booked a flight and now need to modify one part of the trip.

### Task

Find the relevant booking-management option.

### Observe

- Can participants find Manage Booking?
- Can they identify available actions?
- Can they distinguish between free and paid changes?
- Is the current booking context maintained?

### Success Criteria

The participant reaches the appropriate management action without assistance.

---

## 18. Task 11 — Understand Refund and Cancellation

### Scenario

You can no longer travel and want to understand what happens if you cancel the booking.

### Task

Find the cancellation/refund information and determine the expected refund.

### Observe

- Can participants find the cancellation option?
- Do they understand eligibility?
- Can they identify applicable fees?
- Can they understand the expected refund?
- Is confirmation sufficiently explicit?

### Success Criteria

The participant can explain the cancellation consequences before confirming the action.

# 19. Observation Framework

During testing, observations should be recorded using the following structure:

| Observation | Evidence | Severity | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interaction issue | What the participant did/said | Low/Medium/High/Critical | Proposed improvement |
| Information issue | What was misunderstood | Low/Medium/High/Critical | Proposed improvement |
| Navigation issue | Where participant became lost | Low/Medium/High/Critical | Proposed improvement |
| Accessibility issue | Barrier encountered | Low/Medium/High/Critical | Proposed improvement |

---

# 20. Usability Metrics

The following metrics can be recorded during testing.

### Task Completion

Whether the participant successfully completes the assigned task.

### Time on Task

Approximate time required to complete the task.

### Error Count

Number of observable mistakes or incorrect interactions.

### Assistance Required

Whether the facilitator had to provide guidance.

### Confidence

Participant's reported confidence after completing the task.

### Satisfaction

Participant's overall perception of the experience.

---

# 21. Task Success Scale

Use the following scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 4 | Completed independently and confidently |
| 3 | Completed independently with minor hesitation |
| 2 | Completed with significant difficulty or minor assistance |
| 1 | Unable to complete without assistance |
| 0 | Task failed |

This scale should be applied consistently across testing sessions.

---

# 22. Severity Framework

### Critical

Prevents task completion or creates a serious usability/accessibility barrier.

### High

Causes significant confusion, errors, or abandonment risk.

### Medium

Creates noticeable friction but the task remains achievable.

### Low

Minor usability issue that does not significantly affect task completion.
