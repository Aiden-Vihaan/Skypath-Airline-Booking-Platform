# SkyPath — Research

## Research Objective

The research phase was intended to understand the information, decisions, and constraints that shape an airline booking experience.

The objective was not simply to identify preferred visual styles.

Instead, the research focused on:

- user goals;
- decision-making behaviour;
- pain points;
- information requirements;
- traveller differences;
- accessibility considerations;
- booking-stage uncertainty.

---

## Research Questions

The project was guided by the following questions:

1. What information do users need when searching for flights?
2. Which factors influence flight selection?
3. How do users compare competing flight options?
4. When do additional costs become problematic?
5. What information is important when selecting baggage and seats?
6. How do different traveller types change the booking experience?
7. What creates anxiety immediately before payment?
8. Which post-booking tasks need to remain easily accessible?
9. How can accessibility requirements be incorporated into the core booking journey?

---

## Research Themes

The research and design exploration identified several recurring themes.

### Theme 01 — Price Is Not the Only Decision Variable

Users may initially search by price but often evaluate:

- duration;
- departure time;
- arrival time;
- stops;
- baggage;
- flexibility;
- seat availability.

Therefore, a low ticket price does not necessarily represent the best option.

---

### Theme 02 — Users Need Progressive Disclosure

Showing every possible piece of information simultaneously increases cognitive load.

However, hiding important restrictions can create uncertainty.

The design therefore follows a progressive-disclosure approach:

**summary → comparison → details → decision**

Users can understand the essential information first and inspect deeper information when required.

---

### Theme 03 — Cost Transparency Builds Confidence

Additional services can significantly affect the final price.

The booking experience should therefore communicate price changes as users make selections.

This principle influenced the baggage, seat, add-on, review, and payment flows.

---

### Theme 04 — Traveller Context Changes Priorities

Different travellers optimise for different outcomes.

| Traveller | Primary considerations |
|---|---|
| Business | Time, flexibility, efficiency |
| Family | Children, seating, baggage, meals |
| Student | Price, baggage, flexibility |
| Senior | Simplicity, assistance, readability |
| Accessibility-focused | Assistance, accessible facilities, seating |
| Leisure | Price, convenience, destination |

This led to a persona-driven design strategy rather than a one-size-fits-all experience.

---

## Persona Research

Six representative traveller contexts were explored during the project.

### Business Traveller

Needs fast decision-making and predictable travel.

Key concerns:

- schedule;
- flexibility;
- efficient booking;
- seat preference;
- reliable itinerary information.

---

### Family Traveller

Travels with children and requires coordination between multiple passengers.

Key concerns:

- child seating;
- baggage;
- food options;
- accommodation considerations;
- managing multiple passengers.

---

### Student Traveller

Often highly price-sensitive while carrying significant luggage.

Key concerns:

- fare price;
- baggage allowance;
- affordable options;
- flexible changes;
- clear restrictions.

---

### Senior Traveller

May prefer reduced complexity and highly readable information.

Key concerns:

- simple navigation;
- clear language;
- assistance;
- accessibility;
- easy-to-understand booking status.

---

### Accessibility-Focused Traveller

Requires confidence that accessibility requirements can be supported.

Key concerns:

- airport assistance;
- accessible facilities;
- seating;
- mobility support;
- clear communication.

---

## Journey Research

The journey map structured the booking experience into eight stages:

1. Discover
2. Search Flights
3. Compare Options
4. Select Flight & Fare
5. Passenger Details
6. Seat & Add-ons
7. Review & Payment
8. Confirmation & Post-Booking

Each stage was examined through:

- user goal;
- key actions;
- thoughts;
- emotional state;
- pain points;
- design opportunities.

---

## Information Architecture Research

Because airline booking contains multiple interconnected tasks, information architecture became a major part of the research process.

The project explored:

- content inventory;
- card sorting;
- navigation model;
- sitemap;
- task relationships;
- booking-management hierarchy.

The goal was to organise content around user tasks rather than internal business terminology.

---

## Research-to-Design Translation

Research findings were converted into design requirements.

| Research insight | Design response |
|---|---|
| Users compare multiple flight variables | Structured comparison |
| Additional costs create uncertainty | Dynamic price updates |
| Baggage differs by traveller | Explicit baggage breakdown |
| Families coordinate multiple passengers | Passenger-aware booking flow |
| Accessibility information may be critical | Accessible-service visibility |
| Payment is a high-confidence moment | Dedicated review stage |
| Booking continues after payment | Integrated booking management |
| Users have different priorities | Persona-informed experiences |

---

## Research Limitations

The project is a UX design study rather than a large-scale commercial research programme.

The research therefore has limitations:

- limited participant sample;
- concept-stage assumptions;
- no access to proprietary airline analytics;
- no production booking data;
- prototype-based rather than live-product validation.

These limitations were considered when interpreting the findings.

The resulting design should therefore be treated as a validated design direction rather than a claim of universal user behaviour.

---

## Research Outcome

The research phase established a central design principle:

> **Users should be able to understand the consequences of a booking decision before committing to it.**

This principle became a foundation for the information architecture, comparison experience, baggage flow, payment review, and post-booking system.
