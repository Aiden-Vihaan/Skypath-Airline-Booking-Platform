# SkyPath — Case Study Overview

## Project

**SkyPath — Airline Booking Platform**

SkyPath is a UX/UI design concept for an airline booking platform focused on reducing friction across the end-to-end flight-booking experience.

The project explores how information architecture, interaction design, accessibility, transparent pricing, and user-centred decision support can work together to create a more predictable and understandable booking journey.

The design covers the complete journey from flight discovery and search through comparison, passenger information, seat and baggage selection, payment, confirmation, and post-booking management.

---

## Project Objective

The objective of the project was to investigate how an airline booking experience could be redesigned around the actual decisions and concerns users face during travel planning.

Rather than treating booking as a sequence of isolated screens, SkyPath approaches the experience as a connected decision-making system.

The design therefore focuses on:

- reducing unnecessary cognitive load;
- making important information visible at the right moment;
- improving comparison between flight options;
- communicating additional costs clearly;
- supporting different traveller needs;
- providing predictable navigation;
- improving accessibility;
- reducing uncertainty during payment and confirmation;
- supporting users after the booking is completed.

---

The project covers the following major areas:

1. Travel discovery
2. Flight search
3. Flight comparison
4. Flight and fare selection
5. Passenger information
6. Seat selection
7. Baggage and add-ons
8. Review and payment

10. Post-booking management

13. Seat upgrades
14. Check-in
15. Mobile and responsive experiences

---

## Target Users

The experience was designed around multiple traveller contexts rather than a single generic user.

The primary personas include:

- Business Traveller
- Family Traveller
- Student Traveller
- Senior Traveller
- Accessibility-focused Traveller
- Personal / leisure traveller

Each persona introduces different priorities, constraints, and decision-making behaviours.

For example:

- business travellers prioritise efficiency and predictable schedules;
- families require child-friendly options, suitable food, baggage flexibility, and accommodation considerations;
- students are sensitive to price and baggage allowances;
- senior travellers may require simpler navigation and additional assistance;
- accessibility-focused travellers require accessible transport, airport assistance, seating information, and clear communication of accessibility features.

---

## Core Design Principle

The central principle of SkyPath is:

> **Make important travel decisions easier to understand before users commit to them.**

This principle influenced the information architecture, comparison experience, baggage flow, payment experience, and post-booking interactions.

---

## Design Approach

The project followed a structured UX process:

**Research → Personas → Journey Mapping → Information Architecture → User Flows → Wireframes → Interface Design → Prototype → Usability Testing → Iteration → Case Study**

The process was intentionally iterative rather than purely visual.

Design decisions were documented through supporting artefacts including:

- personas;
- UX journey map;
- content inventory;
- card sorting;
- navigation model;
- sitemap;
- user-flow diagrams;
- wireframes;
- interaction map;
- transition specifications;
- prototype testing;
- usability findings;
- recommendations;
- iteration log.

---

## Expected Outcome

The intended outcome was not simply a visually polished airline interface.

The project aims to demonstrate the ability to:

- identify UX problems;
- structure complex information;
- model user behaviour;
- translate research insights into design requirements;
- create coherent interaction flows;
- design for multiple user contexts;
- test and refine a solution;
- communicate design reasoning systematically.

SkyPath therefore functions as a complete UX case study rather than a collection of interface screens.
