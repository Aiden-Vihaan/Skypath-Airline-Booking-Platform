# Persona Summary

## SkyPath — Traveller Persona Framework

The SkyPath personas were created to translate research observations into practical design requirements.

Rather than treating every traveller as having the same priorities, the persona set represents different situations in which an airline booking experience can become difficult, time-consuming or uncertain.

The personas are not intended to describe every possible traveller. They act as design lenses that help evaluate whether the SkyPath experience remains useful across different booking contexts.

---

## 1. Business Traveller

### Primary need
**Speed, reliability and decision efficiency.**

The Business Traveller approaches flight booking as a time-sensitive task. They want to identify a suitable flight quickly without sacrificing important information about schedule, duration, fare conditions or flexibility.

### Key priorities

- Departure and arrival times
- Shorter travel duration
- Reliable flight options
- Clear fare conditions
- Transparent total pricing
- Flexible booking and change options
- Efficient passenger information entry
- Easy access to the itinerary after booking

### Design implication

SkyPath should support rapid comparison and reduce unnecessary interaction while keeping critical decision information visible.

---

## 2. Student Traveller

### Primary need
**Affordability with confidence.**

The Student Traveller is highly conscious of the total cost of travel. However, the cheapest advertised fare is not necessarily the best option if baggage, seating or other additional charges substantially increase the final price.

### Key priorities

- Total price
- Baggage allowance
- Fare inclusions
- Travel duration
- Schedule flexibility
- Comparison clarity
- Avoiding unexpected charges
- Easy access to booking information

### Design implication

SkyPath should make the actual cost and meaningful differences between fares easy to understand before the user commits to payment.

---

## 3. Family Traveller

### Primary need
**Coordinating a comfortable and predictable journey for the whole family.**

Family travel introduces additional complexity, particularly when parents are travelling with infants or young children.

The traveller may need to consider seating, baggage, meals, child-related services, arrival timing and accommodation check-in alongside the flight itself.

### Key priorities

- Keeping family members together
- Child-friendly seating considerations
- Baggage requirements
- Food and meal options
- Child-related services
- Convenient departure and arrival times
- Total family booking cost
- Accommodation check-in and check-out timing
- Clear review and confirmation

### Design implication

SkyPath should support group-level decision making while still maintaining clear information for each individual passenger.

Accommodation is treated as a connected travel-planning consideration rather than as a claim that SkyPath itself provides hotel booking.

---

## 4. Accessibility Persona

### Primary need
**Independent access and predictable interaction.**

The Accessibility Persona is a needs-based persona rather than a representation of one specific disability.

It represents travellers who may encounter barriers when an interface depends heavily on visual perception, precise pointer interaction, complex navigation or poorly communicated system states.

### Key priorities

- Keyboard accessibility
- Screen-reader compatibility
- Clear labels
- Sufficient contrast
- Non-colour indicators
- Predictable navigation
- Accessible forms
- Clear error messages
- Accessible selection states
- Understandable confirmation

### Design implication

Accessibility should be considered throughout the product rather than treated as a final visual adjustment.

SkyPath should allow users to search, compare, enter information, select services, review and confirm a booking with minimal dependence on another person.

---

## 5. Senior Traveller

### Primary need
**Clarity, confidence and reduced complexity.**

The Senior Traveller represents users who may prefer a more deliberate booking experience with strong visual hierarchy, straightforward language and predictable navigation.

This persona does not assume a particular medical condition or level of technical ability.

### Key priorities

- Readable information
- Clear typography
- Simple navigation
- Familiar terminology
- Visible booking progress
- Straightforward forms
- Clear error recovery
- Strong confirmation
- Easy access to support

### Design implication

SkyPath should not equate usability with speed alone.

A successful experience should allow travellers to understand what they have selected, what happens next and whether their booking has been completed successfully.

---

# Shared Needs Across Personas

Although the personas have different priorities, several needs consistently appear across the set.

### 1. Transparent information

Travellers need to understand the real implications of a flight choice before committing.

Important information should not be hidden behind unnecessary steps.

### 2. Clear comparison

Flight options should be comparable using meaningful information such as:

- Price
- Duration
- Stops
- Departure time
- Arrival time
- Fare conditions
- Baggage
- Relevant inclusions

### 3. Reduced uncertainty

Users should always understand:

- Where they are in the process
- What they have selected
- What they are paying for
- What happens next
- Whether an action was successful

### 4. Error prevention

The experience should prevent avoidable mistakes through:

- Clear labels
- Structured forms
- Useful validation
- Review checkpoints
- Understandable error messages

### 5. Post-booking confidence

The experience should not end at payment.

Users should be able to quickly access:

- Booking confirmation
- Itinerary
- Passenger details
- Booking reference
- Relevant post-booking actions

---

# Persona-Driven Design Priorities

The persona framework resulted in five major design priorities for SkyPath:

1. **Comparison clarity**
2. **Transparent pricing**
3. **Low-friction booking**
4. **Inclusive and accessible interaction**
5. **Strong confirmation and post-booking continuity**

These priorities provide a bridge between the research phase and the subsequent information architecture, user flows, wireframes and final interface design.

---

# How the Personas Were Used

The personas were not created as standalone documentation.

They were used as evaluation lenses throughout the design process.

For example:

- **Business Traveller:** Can the user compare flights quickly?
- **Student Traveller:** Can the user understand the real cost?
- **Family Traveller:** Can the user coordinate multiple passengers and child-related needs?
- **Accessibility Persona:** Can the user complete the task without avoidable interaction barriers?
- **Senior Traveller:** Can the user understand the interface without unnecessary complexity?

This approach helps ensure that design decisions are connected to user needs rather than being based solely on visual preference.

---

# Limitations

These personas represent synthesized design hypotheses rather than statistically representative demographic profiles.

They should not be interpreted as evidence that every business, student, family, senior or accessibility-focused traveller behaves in the same way.

Further usability testing and research would be required to validate individual assumptions and identify additional traveller segments.
