# SkyPath — Usability Test Plan

## 1. Study Overview

**Project:** SkyPath — Airline Booking Platform  
**Study Type:** Moderated usability evaluation  
**Research Area:** User experience, interaction design, information architecture, and task efficiency  
**Prototype Fidelity:** High-fidelity interactive prototype  
**Primary Platform:** Responsive web interface  
**Evaluation Stage:** Post-prototype usability validation

The purpose of this study is to evaluate whether the SkyPath airline booking experience enables users to complete common travel-related tasks efficiently, confidently, and with minimal cognitive effort.

The evaluation focuses on the interaction quality of the booking journey rather than visual preference alone. Particular attention is given to flight discovery, comparison, passenger information, baggage selection, seat selection, payment, and post-booking management.

---

## 2. Research Objectives

The study is designed to answer the following questions:

1. Can users understand the overall booking process without additional explanation?
2. Can users successfully search for and compare flights?
3. Can users distinguish between fare types and their associated benefits?
4. Can users understand baggage allowances and additional baggage charges?
5. Can users select seats without uncertainty?
6. Can users review the complete booking cost before payment?
7. Can users recover from mistakes without losing entered information?
8. Can users locate important post-booking functions such as check-in, refund, and booking management?
9. Which interaction points create the greatest cognitive or emotional friction?
10. Which design decisions should be prioritised for the next iteration?

---

## 3. Evaluation Scope

### Included

- Flight search
- Flight filtering and comparison
- Fare selection
- Passenger information
- Baggage and add-ons
- Seat selection
- Booking review
- Payment
- Booking confirmation
- Check-in
- Refund workflow
- Loyalty-related interactions
- Responsive navigation

### Excluded

The following are outside the scope of this evaluation:

- Actual payment processing
- Real airline inventory
- Real-time flight availability
- Production authentication
- Backend performance
- Actual ticket issuance
- Security penetration testing
- Accessibility conformance auditing at production level

These areas require technical or production-level validation beyond the scope of the UX prototype.

---

## 4. Participants

The intended participant pool consists of people who have experience with online travel booking or are representative of potential airline-booking users.

Participant diversity should consider:

- Age
- Frequency of air travel
- Digital confidence
- Device preference
- Travel purpose
- Previous experience with airline websites or applications

Where possible, participants should represent different traveller contexts identified during persona development, including:

- Leisure travellers
- Business travellers
- Family travellers
- Senior travellers
- Travellers with accessibility requirements
- Student travellers

Participant identities and personal information should not be stored in this repository.

---

## 5. Methodology

A task-based moderated usability study will be used.

Each participant will be asked to complete realistic travel scenarios using the SkyPath prototype.

The moderator should avoid guiding participants toward the intended interface or correcting their behaviour during task execution.

Participants should be encouraged to verbalise their expectations and reasoning where appropriate.

The moderator will record:

- Task completion
- Errors
- Misinterpretations
- Navigation behaviour
- Hesitation
- Repeated actions
- Questions asked
- Visible frustration
- Confidence
- Unexpected behaviour
- Qualitative comments

---

## 6. Evaluation Metrics

### Task Completion

Whether the participant successfully completes the intended task.

### Time on Task

Approximate time required to complete each task.

### Error Rate

Number and severity of incorrect actions or recoverable mistakes.

### Navigation Efficiency

Number of unnecessary steps, backtracking actions, or abandoned paths.

### Comprehension

Whether labels, pricing information, fare rules, and system states are correctly understood.

### Confidence

Participant-reported confidence in the decision immediately after completing a task.

### Qualitative Feedback

Participant comments regarding clarity, trust, friction, and perceived usefulness.

---

## 7. Severity Classification

Observed usability problems should be classified using the following scale.

| Severity | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No usability issue |
| 1 | Minor issue with limited impact |
| 2 | Moderate issue causing hesitation or inefficiency |
| 3 | Major issue preventing or seriously disrupting task completion |
| 4 | Critical issue causing task failure or severe loss of trust |

Severity should be assigned based on observed impact rather than subjective preference.

---

## 8. Ethical Considerations

Participation should be voluntary.

Participants should be informed that the evaluation concerns the prototype and not their personal ability.

No unnecessary personally identifiable information should be collected.

Participants should be permitted to stop the session at any point.

If sessions are recorded, explicit consent should be obtained before recording begins.

---

## 9. Data Analysis

Qualitative observations will be grouped into recurring usability themes.

Potential themes include:

- Navigation ambiguity
- Information overload
- Pricing transparency
- Baggage comprehension
- Seat-selection uncertainty
- Form complexity
- Trust and reassurance
- Error recovery
- Accessibility barriers

Quantitative measures should be summarised only after sufficient observations have been collected.

No numerical performance claims should be made from anecdotal observations.

---

## 10. Research Outputs

The usability evaluation will produce:

- Task-level observations
- Usability issue inventory
- Severity ratings
- Cross-participant patterns
- Design recommendations
- Iteration priorities
- Updated interaction decisions

The findings will inform the next design iteration and provide traceability between observed user behaviour and subsequent design changes.
