# Opportunity Gaps

## 1. Purpose

The competitive analysis identified several areas where existing flight-booking experiences can still create friction for travellers.

This document translates those observations into opportunity gaps for the SkyPath product.

The purpose is not to claim that existing platforms are poorly designed.

Instead, the objective is to identify areas where SkyPath can establish a more focused and user-centred experience.

---

# 2. Gap 01 — Comparison Does Not Always Equal Understanding

Most major travel platforms provide extensive comparison information.

However, providing more information does not automatically make the decision easier.

Users may still need to determine:

- Which flight is best for their schedule
- Which fare provides better value
- Whether a cheaper flight has meaningful restrictions
- Whether additional baggage changes the effective cost
- Whether a longer journey is worth the savings

### Opportunity

SkyPath can move from simple comparison toward **decision-oriented comparison**.

The interface should help users understand why one option may be more suitable than another.

---

# 3. Gap 02 — Price Versus Effective Cost

The displayed ticket price is not always the final cost of travelling.

Additional costs can include:

- Baggage
- Seat selection
- Optional services
- Fare upgrades
- Other booking-related charges

### Opportunity

SkyPath can make the relationship between the initial fare and the effective booking cost more visible.

The user should be able to understand:

**Base fare**

+

**Required charges**

+

**Selected extras**

=

**Final cost**

---

# 4. Gap 03 — Fare Complexity

Airlines commonly provide multiple fare families.

The differences may involve:

- Flexibility
- Cancellation
- Baggage
- Seat selection
- Changes
- Other benefits

### Problem

Users may understand the price difference without understanding the practical difference.

### Opportunity

SkyPath can provide structured fare comparison that answers:

- What is included?
- What is restricted?
- What does the upgrade provide?
- Is the additional cost worthwhile?

---

# 5. Gap 04 — Information Overload

Flight booking involves a large amount of information.

Users may need to process:

- Flight schedules
- Airports
- Stops
- Duration
- Prices
- Fare rules
- Baggage
- Seats
- Services
- Passenger requirements

### Problem

Showing everything simultaneously increases cognitive load.

### Opportunity

SkyPath can use:

- Progressive disclosure
- Clear hierarchy
- Grouped information
- Expandable details
- Contextual explanations

The objective is not to reduce information, but to present the right information at the right moment.

---

# 6. Gap 05 — Choice Overload

Flexible search can provide users with hundreds of possible combinations.

More choices can become counterproductive when users cannot easily narrow them down.

### Opportunity

SkyPath can provide meaningful filtering and sorting based on traveller priorities.

Possible decision dimensions include:

- Lowest price
- Shortest journey
- Fewest stops
- Preferred departure time
- Best overall value
- Flexible fare

---

# 7. Gap 06 — Search-to-Booking Discontinuity

Metasearch platforms can provide excellent discovery and comparison experiences but may redirect users to another provider for booking.

### Problem

This creates a transition between:

**Comparison environment**

and

**Booking environment**

The user may need to re-establish context.

### Opportunity

SkyPath can maintain a continuous experience between:

**Search → Comparison → Selection → Booking**

---

# 8. Gap 07 — Repetitive Passenger Information

Passenger information can involve significant data entry.

Repeatedly entering:

- Name
- Contact information
- Travel-document information
- Preferences

can increase friction.

### Opportunity

SkyPath can support reusable traveller profiles while maintaining user control over stored information.

---

# 9. Gap 08 — Optional Services Can Complicate the Journey

Seats, baggage and other services are valuable, but they introduce additional decisions.

### Problem

Users may struggle to distinguish:

**Required**

from

**Optional**

### Opportunity

SkyPath can separate essential booking requirements from optional services.

Optional additions should also clearly communicate their effect on the total cost.

---

# 10. Gap 09 — Booking Confirmation Is Often Treated as the Endpoint

Payment is frequently treated as the final stage of the booking journey.

However, travellers continue to need access to their reservation.

### Post-booking needs may include:

- Itinerary
- Booking reference
- Flight status
- Seat
- Baggage
- Check-in
- Travel information

### Opportunity

SkyPath can treat the booking as a persistent travel object.

---

# 11. Gap 10 — Decision Confidence

A traveller may successfully complete a booking without feeling completely confident about the decision.

Confidence depends on whether users understand:

- What they selected
- Why they selected it
- What they are paying
- What is included
- What happens next

### Opportunity

SkyPath can make **decision confidence** a core UX objective.

---

# 12. Gap 11 — Context Preservation

Users make decisions across multiple screens.

If important information disappears between stages, users may need to remember previous decisions.

### Opportunity

SkyPath should preserve critical context throughout the journey.

Examples include:

- Selected flight
- Travel dates
- Passenger count
- Fare
- Total cost

---

# 13. Gap 12 — Post-Booking Continuity

The travel journey extends beyond booking.

Users may later need to:

- Review their itinerary
- Modify services
- Check flight status
- Prepare for departure
- Access travel information

### Opportunity

The SkyPath experience can extend beyond payment into a lightweight travel-management layer.

# 15. Strategic Opportunity Areas

The identified gaps can be grouped into four larger themes.

## A. Decision Support

- Meaningful comparison
- Fare explanation
- Effective-cost visibility
- Recommendation logic

## B. Cognitive Load Reduction

- Progressive disclosure
- Information hierarchy
- Simplified choices
- Context preservation

## C. Booking Efficiency

- Reusable passenger information
- Structured forms
- Clear validation
- Continuous booking flow

## D. Travel Continuity

- Confirmation
- Persistent itinerary
- Booking management
- Travel preparation

---

# 16. SkyPath Opportunity Statement

The competitive research suggests an opportunity to design a flight-booking experience that does not simply provide more choices.

Instead, SkyPath should help users **understand, compare and act on those choices with confidence**.

The opportunity can therefore be expressed as:

> **How might we help travellers move from a large set of flight options to a confident booking decision without unnecessary cognitive effort?**

---

# 17. Design Response

The opportunity gaps will inform the following SkyPath design decisions:

1. Structured flight comparison
2. Transparent fare cards
3. Effective-cost visibility
4. Persistent itinerary context
5. Progressive disclosure
6. Clear optional-service separation
7. Reusable traveller information
8. Simplified passenger forms
9. Strong booking review
10. Persistent post-booking access

---

# 18. Validation Requirement

These opportunity gaps represent design hypotheses derived from competitive analysis.

They should be validated through primary user research and usability testing.

The next research phase should therefore investigate whether real travellers experience these problems and which gaps have the greatest impact on their booking behaviour.

---

# 19. Conclusion

The strongest opportunity is not to outperform competitors by adding more functionality.

It is to create a more coherent relationship between:

**Information**

→

**Comparison**

→

**Understanding**

→

**Decision**

→

**Booking**

→

**Management**

SkyPath can differentiate itself by reducing uncertainty at each of these transitions.
