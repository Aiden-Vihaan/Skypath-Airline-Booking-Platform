# Heuristic Evaluation

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this heuristic evaluation is to examine the competitive flight-booking experiences from a usability and interaction-design perspective.

The evaluation focuses on the five competitors previously analysed:

- Booking.com
- Google Flights
- Skyscanner
- Emirates
- Qatar Airways

The objective is not to declare one platform as universally better than another.

Instead, the evaluation identifies recurring interaction patterns, usability strengths, potential friction points and opportunities that can inform the SkyPath design.

The evaluation uses established usability principles, particularly Nielsen's usability heuristics, together with domain-specific considerations relevant to flight booking.

---

# 2. Evaluation Framework

The following principles were used as the evaluation framework.

## H1 — Visibility of System Status

The interface should continuously communicate what is happening.

Examples include:

- Search progress
- Loading states
- Selected flight
- Booking progress
- Payment status
- Confirmation status

---

## H2 — Match Between System and the Real World

The interface should use language and concepts familiar to travellers.

Examples include:

- Departure
- Arrival
- Baggage
- Seat
- Boarding
- Fare
- Passenger

---

## H3 — User Control and Freedom

Users should be able to:

- Modify search criteria
- Go back
- Change selected options
- Remove optional services
- Review previous decisions

without unnecessarily restarting the booking process.

---

## H4 — Consistency and Standards

Similar concepts should behave and appear consistently.

Examples include:

- Button behaviour
- Form fields
- Fare cards
- Navigation
- Error messages
- Selection states

---

## H5 — Error Prevention

The system should prevent avoidable mistakes before they occur.

Examples include:

- Invalid passenger information
- Incorrect dates
- Incompatible selections
- Missing mandatory information
- Accidental purchase actions

---

## H6 — Recognition Rather Than Recall

Users should not have to remember information from previous screens.

Important decisions should remain visible or easily accessible.

---

## H7 — Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

The experience should support both:

- First-time users
- Experienced or returning users

Examples include:

- Saved traveller information
- Autofill
- Saved searches
- Flexible dates
- Price alerts

---

## H8 — Aesthetic and Minimalist Design

The interface should avoid unnecessary information competing with the primary task.

Information should be prioritised according to user needs.

---

## H9 — Help Users Recognise and Recover from Errors

When something goes wrong, the system should explain:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- What the user can do next

---

## H10 — Help and Documentation

Important information should be available when users need it.

Examples include:

- Fare rules
- Baggage policies
- Cancellation conditions
- Travel requirements
- Booking support

---

# 3. Domain-Specific Evaluation Criteria

Flight booking requires additional evaluation beyond general usability.

The following domain-specific criteria were therefore considered.

### Price Transparency

Can users understand the effective cost of the booking?

### Fare Transparency

Can users distinguish between fare types and their restrictions?

### Travel-Option Comparison

Can users compare flights efficiently?

### Baggage Visibility

Can users understand what baggage is included?

### Decision Continuity

Does the interface preserve important decisions throughout the booking process?

### Booking Confidence

Can users verify that the final purchase matches their intended itinerary?

### Post-Booking Continuity

Can users easily access and manage the booking after payment?

---

The following qualitative scale is used.

| Rating | Interpretation |
|---|---|

| 4 | Strong support |
| 3 | Adequate support |

# 11. Comparative Interpretation

The matrix highlights several patterns.

## Search Platforms

Google Flights and Skyscanner demonstrate particularly strong support for flexibility and exploration.

Their primary strength is helping users navigate a large search space.

---

## Marketplace Platforms

Booking.com demonstrates the importance of connecting discovery with transaction and subsequent booking-related interaction.

Its broader marketplace model requires the interface to manage a large amount of information.

---

## Direct Airline Platforms

Emirates and Qatar Airways demonstrate stronger continuity between:

**Flight**

→ **Fare**

→ **Passenger**

→ **Services**

→ **Payment**

→ **Booking**

Their main advantage is control over the complete transaction ecosystem.

# 12. Recurring Competitive Strengths

Across the five competitors, several strong patterns repeatedly appear.

### 12.1 Search Flexibility

Users increasingly expect the ability to modify dates, destinations and other constraints.

### 12.2 Transparent Comparison

Price alone is insufficient.

Users need supporting information to evaluate value.

### 12.3 Progressive Decision-Making

Complex bookings become easier when decisions are divided into manageable stages.

### 12.4 Persistent Booking Information

Important itinerary details should remain accessible throughout the journey.

### 12.5 Post-Booking Access

The relationship continues after payment.

---

# 13. Recurring UX Risks

Several potential risks also appear across the competitive landscape.

## Information Overload

Travel booking requires large amounts of information.

Poor hierarchy can make comparison difficult.

---

## Fare Complexity

Similar-looking fares may contain significantly different restrictions and benefits.

---

## Ancillary Complexity

Baggage, seats and additional services can make the total cost difficult to understand.

---

## Provider Handoffs

Metasearch platforms can require users to move to another provider to complete the transaction.

---

## Choice Overload

Flexible search can create more possibilities than users can comfortably evaluate.

---

## Post-Booking Fragmentation

Important travel information may become distributed across emails, airline platforms and travel-provider systems.

---

# 14. SkyPath Opportunity Areas

The competitive evaluation suggests six major opportunity areas.

## Opportunity 01 — Decision-Centred Comparison

Instead of showing only more information, SkyPath should help users understand which information matters.

---

## Opportunity 02 — Transparent Fare Architecture

Fare differences should be understandable without requiring users to interpret complex pricing structures.

---

## Opportunity 03 — Persistent Context

The selected itinerary should remain visible or easily accessible throughout the booking journey.

---

## Opportunity 04 — Reduced Repetitive Input

Traveller information should be reusable wherever appropriate.

---

## Opportunity 05 — Clear Optional Services

Optional additions should be clearly separated from required booking decisions.

---

## Opportunity 06 — Post-Booking Continuity

The booking should remain accessible as a persistent travel object after payment.

---

# 16. Design Principles Derived from Evaluation

The competitive evaluation translates into the following SkyPath design principles.

### Principle 01

**Make the important information visible before the user needs to search for it.**

### Principle 02

**Explain the difference between options, not just the difference in price.**

### Principle 03

**Preserve user decisions across screens.**

### Principle 04

**Separate mandatory information from optional additions.**

### Principle 05

**Prevent errors before payment whenever possible.**

### Principle 06

**Use progressive disclosure to manage information density.**

### Principle 07

**Allow flexibility without forcing flexibility on every user.**

### Principle 08

**Treat confirmation as the beginning of post-booking support.**

# 18. What SkyPath Should Avoid

SkyPath should avoid simply accumulating features from competitors.

The competitive analysis indicates that more functionality does not automatically create a better experience.

The following patterns should be avoided:

- Excessive information density
- Unexplained fare differences
- Hidden additional costs
- Unnecessary form repetition
- Aggressive optional-service presentation
- Excessive choice without guidance
- Loss of context between booking stages
- Unclear post-booking pathways

---

# 19. Final Heuristic Insight

The most important finding from the evaluation is that flight booking is not a single decision.

It is a sequence of interconnected decisions:

**Where should I go?**

→

**When should I travel?**

→

**Which flight is suitable?**

→

**Which fare provides the right value?**

→

**What services do I need?**

→

**Is the final price acceptable?**

→

**Is everything correct?**

→

**How do I manage the trip afterwards?**

A strong booking experience should preserve the relationship between these decisions.

---

# 20. SkyPath UX Hypothesis

Based on the competitive evaluation, the central SkyPath hypothesis is:

> **If travellers are given clear comparison information, transparent fare trade-offs, persistent booking context and a predictable end-to-end journey, they will be able to make booking decisions with greater confidence and less cognitive effort.**

This hypothesis will be validated through the subsequent design process and usability evaluation.

---

# 21. Evaluation Limitation

This heuristic evaluation is a design-analysis exercise rather than a controlled usability study.

The ratings are qualitative assessments based on competitive product analysis and documented interaction patterns.

They should not be interpreted as empirical usability scores.

Future validation should include:

- Task-based usability testing
- Think-aloud sessions
- User interviews
- Comparative usability testing
- Accessibility testing
- Completion-time analysis
- Error-rate analysis
- User satisfaction measures
