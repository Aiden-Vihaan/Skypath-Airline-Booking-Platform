# SkyPath — User Goals

## 1. Purpose

User goals define what travellers are trying to accomplish before, during and after an airline booking.

For SkyPath, these goals are considered at the experience level rather than being limited to individual interface actions.

The objective is to understand what successful completion means from the traveller's perspective.

---

## 2. Primary User Goal

> **Complete an appropriate flight booking with a clear understanding of the available options, total cost, selected services and next steps.**

The primary goal combines functional completion with confidence and understanding.

A booking should not be considered successful merely because payment has been completed. The user should also understand what they have purchased and how to access it afterwards.

---

## 3. Discovery Goals

During the initial search stage, users want to:

- Define their origin and destination.
- Select appropriate travel dates.
- Specify the number of travellers.
- Find flights relevant to their requirements.
- Quickly understand the available options.
- Return to or modify previous searches when necessary.

### Desired Outcome

Users should reach the search-results stage without unnecessary interaction or uncertainty.

---

## 4. Comparison Goals

When evaluating available flights, users want to:

- Compare prices.
- Compare travel duration.
- Understand the number of stops.
- Understand baggage allowances.
- Identify important fare differences.
- Evaluate convenience against price.
- Identify the option that best matches their priorities.

### Desired Outcome

Users should be able to distinguish meaningful differences between flights without repeatedly opening and closing multiple screens.

---

## 5. Selection Goals

After comparing available flights, users want to:

- Understand the selected flight.
- Review important flight details.
- Understand fare conditions.
- Identify what is included.
- Identify what is optional.
- Select an appropriate fare.
- Change their selection if necessary.

### Desired Outcome

Users should be able to make a selection with sufficient context to understand its consequences.

---

## 6. Passenger Information Goals

During passenger information entry, users want to:

- Provide the required traveller information.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Understand which fields are mandatory.
- Correct mistakes before proceeding.
- Review entered information.

### Desired Outcome

Users should be able to complete passenger information accurately with minimal unnecessary effort.

---

## 7. Seat Selection Goals

When selecting seats, users want to:

- Understand available seating options.
- Identify their selected seat.
- Understand relevant seat conditions or costs.
- Change their selection when necessary.
- Confirm the final seat assignment.

### Desired Outcome

Users should feel confident that the seat shown during review matches the seat they selected.

---

## 8. Optional Service Goals

When additional services are offered, users want to:

- Understand which services are optional.
- Understand the cost of each service.
- Distinguish included services from paid additions.
- Add services that are genuinely useful.
- Decline unnecessary additions without friction.

### Desired Outcome

Users should remain in control of optional purchases rather than feeling pressured or confused by them.

---

## 9. Review Goals

Before payment, users want to:

- Review the selected flight.
- Review fare details.
- Review passenger information.
- Review seat selection.
- Review optional services.
- Understand the total price.
- Identify mistakes before payment.
- Make corrections without restarting the entire booking.

### Desired Outcome

The review stage should act as a confidence checkpoint before the final transaction.

---

## 10. Payment Goals

During payment, users want to:

- Understand the final amount.
- Know what they are paying for.
- Enter payment information securely.
- Receive clear feedback about the transaction.
- Understand whether the payment was successful.

### Desired Outcome

Users should not be left uncertain about the status of their transaction.

---

## 11. Confirmation Goals

After completing payment, users want to:

- Receive clear confirmation.
- Access their booking reference.
- Review the confirmed itinerary.
- Understand what to do next.
- Access relevant booking information.

### Desired Outcome

The confirmation stage should provide reassurance and a clear transition from booking to travel preparation.

---

## 12. Post-Booking Goals

After completing a booking, users may want to:

- Access their itinerary.
- Review booking details.
- View boarding information.
- Check flight status.
- Manage eligible booking details.
- Access relevant travel information.

### Desired Outcome

Users should be able to continue managing their journey without having to reconstruct or repeat information from the original booking.

---

## 13. Emotional Goals

Beyond functional requirements, SkyPath considers several emotional goals.

Users should ideally feel:

### Confident

They understand their decisions and the resulting booking.

### Informed

Important information is available when it is needed.

### In Control

Users can review and modify relevant choices.

### Reassured

Important actions provide clear system feedback.

### Unburdened

The interface reduces unnecessary cognitive and interaction effort.

---

## 14. Goal Prioritisation

| Goal | Priority | Rationale |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Find an appropriate flight | Critical | Core purpose of the product |
| Compare available options | Critical | Supports informed selection |
| Understand total cost | Critical | Prevents pricing uncertainty |
| Select an appropriate fare | Critical | Directly affects booking outcome |
| Complete passenger information | Critical | Required for booking completion |
| Review selections | Critical | Important error-prevention stage |
| Complete payment | Critical | Required to complete transaction |
| Receive confirmation | Critical | Establishes booking certainty |
| Manage booking | High | Extends the experience beyond purchase |
| Select optional services | Medium | Adds value but is not essential |
| Personalise preferences | Future | Potential enhancement beyond current scope |

---

## 15. User Goal Hierarchy

The goals can be understood as a progression:

**Find → Compare → Understand → Decide → Configure → Review → Pay → Confirm → Manage**

Each stage supports the next.

A breakdown at an earlier stage can increase uncertainty later in the journey. For this reason, SkyPath treats the booking experience as a connected system rather than a collection of independent screens.

---

## 16. Design Implications

The user goals establish several implications for the product design:

- Important information should be visible at decision points.
- Comparison should support multiple meaningful criteria.
- Price should remain understandable throughout the journey.
- Essential and optional decisions should be clearly differentiated.
- Forms should minimise unnecessary interaction effort.
- Users should be able to review important selections before committing.
- System status should remain visible.
- Confirmation should provide clear next steps.
- Post-booking functionality should maintain continuity with the original booking journey.

---

## 17. Definition of a Successful User Experience

From the user's perspective, a successful SkyPath experience means:

> **The traveller can find a suitable flight, understand the trade-offs, complete the necessary decisions, confirm the total cost and finish the booking without unnecessary uncertainty or interaction effort.**

The experience should support both **task completion** and **informed decision-making**.

That distinction is central to the SkyPath design approach.
