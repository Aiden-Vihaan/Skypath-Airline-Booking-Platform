# SkyPath Airline Booking Platform — Behance Case Study

## Project Overview

**Project:** SkyPath — Airline Booking Platform  
**Discipline:** UX Research, UX Design, Interaction Design, UI Design  
**Role:** UX/UI Designer  
**Project Type:** End-to-end conceptual product design  
**Platform:** Responsive web and mobile experience  
**Focus:** Flight booking, passenger management, baggage, seat selection, loyalty, refunds, accessibility, and travel management

SkyPath is a user-centered airline booking platform designed to make the flight-booking experience clearer, more predictable, and less stressful.

The project explores how a complex airline booking process can be transformed into a structured digital experience by combining user research, information architecture, user flows, wireframing, interaction design, accessibility principles, visual design, prototyping, and usability evaluation.

The goal was not simply to create visually polished booking screens, but to understand the problems users encounter throughout the journey and design a system that supports different passenger needs.

---

# 01. The Challenge

Airline booking interfaces often contain a large amount of information and a high number of decisions.

Users may need to:

- Search and compare flights
- Understand fare differences
- Enter passenger information
- Select baggage
- Choose seats
- Manage special requirements
- Review additional charges
- Make payments
- Modify or cancel bookings
- Request refunds
- Manage loyalty benefits

These interactions become particularly challenging when users have different needs.

A business traveller may prioritize speed and flexibility.

A family traveller may prioritize children, baggage, food, and accommodation-related information.

A senior traveller may require a simpler interface and clearer guidance.

A traveller with accessibility requirements may need reliable information about assistance and accessible services.

Therefore, the central design question became:

> **How might an airline booking platform reduce cognitive load while still providing the flexibility and information required by different types of travellers?**

---

# 02. Design Objectives

The project was guided by five primary objectives.

### 01 — Reduce Cognitive Load

Present complex booking information progressively instead of overwhelming users with everything at once.

### 02 — Improve Transparency

Make pricing, baggage allowances, fare conditions, and booking changes easier to understand.

### 03 — Support Diverse Travellers

Design for different passenger contexts rather than assuming a single ideal user.

### 04 — Create a Consistent Experience

Use a reusable information architecture, design system, and interaction model across the platform.

### 05 — Improve Confidence

Help users understand what they are selecting before they commit to payment or other irreversible actions.

---

# 03. Understanding the Users

The research and persona phase identified several representative traveller groups.

The primary personas included:

- Business Traveller
- Family Traveller
- Student Traveller
- Senior Traveller
- Accessibility-focused Traveller

Each persona represents a different combination of goals, constraints, expectations, and potential pain points.

Rather than treating these personas as isolated user types, they were used to identify recurring experience requirements.

### Common Requirements

Across the personas, several themes emerged:

- Clear pricing
- Simple navigation
- Flexible booking options
- Transparent baggage information
- Easy passenger management
- Reliable seat-selection information
- Accessible interaction patterns
- Clear confirmation states
- Easy booking management

The Family Traveller persona additionally emphasized:

- Travelling with small children
- Child-friendly booking information
- Food requirements
- Accommodation-related concerns
- Family seating
- Baggage requirements
- Reducing complexity for parents travelling with children

# 04. Information Architecture

The information architecture was structured around the user's mental model rather than the organisation of internal airline operations.

The major experience areas include:

1. Flight Search
2. Flight Selection
3. Passenger Information
4. Baggage & Add-ons
5. Seat Selection
6. Payment
7. Booking Confirmation
8. Manage Booking
9. Check-in
10. Loyalty
11. Refunds
12. Travel Support

The architecture was designed to maintain a predictable relationship between primary navigation, task-specific flows, and supporting information.

This structure also provides a foundation for scaling the product beyond the initial booking journey.

---

# 05. User Flows

The project documents several important task flows.

### Flight Booking Flow

Search → Compare Flights → Select Flight → Enter Passenger Details → Baggage → Seat Selection → Review → Payment → Confirmation

### Baggage Flow

Booking → Baggage & Add-ons → Review Included Allowance → Add Additional Baggage → Review Baggage Details → Price Update → Confirm → Booking Review

### Seat Upgrade Flow

Booking → Seat Selection → View Available Seats → Compare Seat Types → Select Seat/Upgrade → Price Update → Confirm

### Refund Flow

Manage Booking → Refund Request → Review Eligibility → Select Passenger/Flight → Refund Summary → Confirm Request → Refund Status

### Loyalty Flow

Profile → Loyalty → View Points → Benefits → Eligible Rewards → Redeem/Apply Benefit

These flows were used as the foundation for interaction design and prototype development.

# 06. Wireframing

Wireframes were developed before committing to high-fidelity visual styling.

The purpose of the wireframing stage was to evaluate:

- Content hierarchy
- Navigation
- Task sequencing
- Information density
- Decision points
- Error prevention
- Responsive behaviour

Desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts were considered as part of the same system rather than treating mobile as a reduced desktop interface.
